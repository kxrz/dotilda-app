I built a journaling app that doesn’t want your attention.

It’s called Dotilda. Each day, she asks one small question. You answer in a line, a word, or just the weather inside. Your answer becomes a dot in your year. Then she stays quiet until tomorrow.

She was born on the e‑ink screen of the Mudita Kompakt, a phone made for switching off. Today she runs on any Android 11+ phone, tablet or e‑reader.

The carousel makes the case for six principles, one per slide:
1. One question a day. Under two minutes, then the rest of the day is yours.
2. A line is plenty. No blank page.
3. Miss a day? Nothing breaks. No streak, no red, no guilt.
4. Yours alone. No account, no analytics, nothing leaves the phone.
5. Calm on any screen. Black on white, large type, no endless scrolling.
6. Free, for good. Open source, Apache 2.0.

Number 3 took the most work. Almost every habit app runs on the streak: it brings you back, then makes you feel guilty the day you break it. Dotilda does the opposite. An empty day becomes a tiny grey dot in the grid. Come back after a week and she says: “There you are. Your sky kept your place.”

An app that doesn’t count your absences is still rare. It shouldn’t be.

We don’t measure usage inside the app either. The only numbers are GitHub’s public download counters, shown as they are on dotilda.com/stats.

It’s a public beta: it works every day, and every piece of feedback helps the next version. You can try Dotilda right on dotilda.com, nothing to install, before downloading the APK.
